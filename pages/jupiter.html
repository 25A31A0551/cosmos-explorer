Jupiter (♃)

The fifth planet from the Sun and the largest planet in the Solar System.

Gas Giant Structure & Atmosphere

Jupiter is composed mainly of hydrogen and helium. Under the enormous pressures deep inside the planet, hydrogen transitions into a liquid metallic state, which contributes to Jupiter's powerful magnetic field and enormous magnetosphere.

Its atmosphere contains prominent cloud bands produced by powerful jet streams and zonal winds. Jupiter's famous Great Red Spot is a massive anticyclonic storm that has been observed for centuries.

Jupiter began forming in the outer regions of the young Solar System where temperatures were low enough for large quantities of ice and rock to accumulate. A growing solid core eventually became massive enough to attract enormous amounts of surrounding hydrogen and helium gas. The result was a giant planet containing more material than all the other planets combined. Jupiter's formation probably happened relatively quickly compared with geological timescales because it had to gather its atmosphere before the young Sun's solar wind cleared much of the remaining disk. Its immense gravity later influenced the paths of asteroids, comets, and smaller bodies throughout the Solar System.

Jupiter is a world without a conventional solid surface. Beneath its colorful cloud bands, pressure increases until hydrogen behaves in increasingly exotic ways. Deep inside, hydrogen may become metallic, allowing electricity to flow and helping generate the planet's powerful magnetic field. Jupiter's atmosphere contains enormous storms, including the famous Great Red Spot, a long-lived atmospheric vortex larger than Earth. Lightning flashes through its clouds, while powerful jet streams move around the planet. Jupiter rotates extremely rapidly, completing one rotation in roughly ten hours, causing its enormous atmosphere to stretch into visible bands around the planet.

The darker side of Jupiter is its immense radiation environment and gravitational power. Its magnetic field traps charged particles and creates one of the harshest radiation environments in the Solar System. Spacecraft approaching its moons must carefully manage radiation exposure. Yet Jupiter's gravity may also have protected the inner Solar System by altering the trajectories of some comets and asteroids, although its exact role is complex. The planet's large moons, especially Europa, Ganymede, Io, and Callisto, form an extraordinary miniature planetary system. Europa may contain a global ocean beneath its icy shell, making the Jupiter system one of the most fascinating places to search for environments where life could potentially exist.

Planetary Metrics

Metric Data
Mean Distance from Sun 778,570,000 km (5.204 AU)
Equatorial Radius 71,492 km (11.2 Earths)
Mass 1.8982 × 1027 kg (317.8 Earths)
Surface Gravity 24.79 m/s2 (2.528 g)
Orbital Period 11.86 Earth Years
Rotation Period Approximately 9h 56m (Fastest planetary rotation)
Known Moons Multiple moons, including Io, Europa, Ganymede and Callisto
