Saturn (♄)

The sixth planet from the Sun, famous for its extensive and bright planetary ring system.

Rings & Planetary Properties

Saturn is a gas giant primarily composed of Hydrogen and Helium. It possesses a mean density of 0.687 g/cm3, making it the only planet in the Solar System less dense than liquid water.

Its rings consist predominantly of water ice particles with minor tholin impurities, ranging in size from micrometers to meters. Key ring divisions include the Cassini Division and Encke Gap, shaped gravitationally by shepherd moons.

Saturn formed in the cold outer region of the Solar System where ice, rock, and gas were abundant. Like Jupiter, it probably began with a growing core that eventually captured a massive hydrogen-helium atmosphere. Saturn became a gas giant but remained less dense than Jupiter and dramatically larger than Earth. Its internal structure is dominated by deep layers of compressed hydrogen, while enormous pressures and temperatures exist far below the visible clouds. The planet's formation also created a huge family of moons and ring material that continue to interact gravitationally. Saturn therefore behaves less like a single planet and more like a complete planetary system.

The most recognizable feature of Saturn is its magnificent ring system. The rings are composed primarily of water-ice particles mixed with smaller amounts of rocky material and dust. Some particles are microscopic while others can be many meters across. The rings contain gaps, waves, ripples, and structures produced by the gravitational influence of Saturn's moons. Saturn's atmosphere contains pale bands, powerful winds, lightning storms, and occasional enormous disturbances. Near its north pole, a strange six-sided atmospheric pattern called the hexagon surrounds the polar region, making Saturn one of the most visually unusual worlds in the Solar System.

The mysterious side of Saturn is hidden beneath its beauty. The planet has an enormous magnetic environment and powerful radiation belts, while its atmosphere contains violent weather systems that can stretch around the planet. Saturn also possesses an extraordinary collection of moons. Titan has a thick atmosphere and lakes and rivers of liquid hydrocarbons, while Enceladus shoots water-rich material into space from fractures near its south pole. These discoveries transformed Saturn from a planet famous mainly for rings into a major destination for astrobiology. Beneath Enceladus's ice may exist a global ocean, making this distant system one of the most promising environments for studying potentially habitable chemistry.
