Mars (♂)

The fourth planet from the Sun, named the Red Planet due to iron oxide minerals on its surface.

Geology & Surface Features

Mars is a terrestrial planet with a thin atmosphere composed primarily of carbon dioxide (CO2). It hosts Olympus Mons, one of the largest known volcanoes in the Solar System, and Valles Marineris, an enormous canyon system extending thousands of kilometres across the planet.

Geological evidence shows that ancient Mars once had flowing liquid water on its surface, including river valleys and lake basins. Today, water is found primarily as water ice beneath the surface and in the polar regions.

Mars formed more than four billion years ago from rocky material orbiting the young Sun. During its early history, Mars was probably warmer and wetter than the cold desert world seen today. Geological evidence reveals ancient valleys, channels, minerals, and sedimentary deposits that indicate liquid water once interacted with the surface. Mars also experienced enormous volcanic activity, producing Olympus Mons, the largest known volcano in the Solar System. Its crust preserves ancient regions that have remained relatively unchanged for billions of years, making Mars a geological time capsule. The planet's reddish appearance comes largely from iron minerals in the soil that reacted with oxygen and became oxidized.

The modern Martian environment is harsh and extremely dry. The atmosphere is mostly carbon dioxide and is far thinner than Earth's, providing little protection against temperature changes and radiation. Dust storms can grow enormously and sometimes cover much of the planet, reducing sunlight and threatening solar-powered spacecraft. Temperatures can fall dramatically at night, particularly near the poles. Mars also has polar ice caps containing water ice and frozen carbon dioxide. Underneath the surface, scientists continue searching for evidence of ancient environments that could once have supported microbial life. The possibility of underground water or brines remains one of the reasons Mars receives such intense scientific attention.

The mysterious side of Mars is connected to its lost habitability. Something transformed it from a world capable of maintaining surface water into a cold, thin-atmosphered desert. The disappearance of much of its global magnetic field may have allowed the solar wind to gradually erode its atmosphere over immense periods of time. Robotic explorers have discovered ancient riverbeds, organic molecules, unusual rocks, and chemically interesting environments. Rovers such as Curiosity and Perseverance continue investigating the planet, while orbiters map its surface and atmosphere. Mars remains one of the strongest candidates for future human exploration and one of the most important worlds for studying whether life ever existed beyond Earth.
