SOLAR SYSTEM

Our Planetary Neighborhood

The Solar System contains the Sun, eight planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids, comets and countless smaller objects.

Mercury

The smallest planet and the closest planet to the Sun. Its heavily cratered surface records billions of years of impacts.

EXPLORE MERCURY

Venus

A world hidden beneath thick clouds and possessing an extreme greenhouse atmosphere.

Earth

Our home world, covered by oceans and possessing the known conditions necessary for complex life.

Mars

A cold desert world with ancient valleys, enormous volcanoes and evidence of ancient water activity.

Jupiter

The largest planet in the Solar System, dominated by hydrogen, helium and enormous atmospheric storms.

Saturn

A gas giant famous for its extensive ring system made from ice, rock and dust particles.

Uranus

An ice giant with a dramatically tilted rotation axis and a blue-green atmosphere containing methane.

Neptune

The most distant recognized planet in the Solar System, with powerful winds and a deep blue atmosphere.

Pluto

A dwarf planet in the Kuiper Belt with mountains of water ice and a famous heart-shaped region.

COSMIC PHYSICS

Beyond The Solar System

Explore concepts that describe the universe at its largest scales and the most extreme physical environments.

Big Bang

The early hot, dense state from which the observable universe has expanded and evolved.

Black Hole

An extreme gravitational region from which nothing that crosses the event horizon can escape back to distant observers.

Wormholes

Hypothetical structures that arise in certain mathematical solutions of spacetime geometry.

String Theory

A theoretical framework in which fundamental entities are modeled as extremely small vibrating strings.

Time Relativity

Relativity predicts that elapsed time depends on relative motion and gravitational environment.

Spacetime

Space and time form a four-dimensional geometric structure whose curvature is related to gravity.

Theia

A hypothesized ancient planetary body involved in the giant-impact scenario for the formation of the Moon.

Origin of Life

Explore scientific hypotheses about how chemistry on the early Earth may have transitioned toward biological systems.

Investigate neutron stars, quasars, pulsars, dark matter, exoplanets and other extraordinary astronomical phenomena.
